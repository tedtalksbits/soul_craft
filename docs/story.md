# Story bible

The world, the cast, the arc and how the story is told. A **draft for the owner to tweak**: anything here can
change, but text in the game (signs, item and boss descriptions, dialogue) should agree with it. When it changes,
update this file first, then the game text.

Status: draft 2, 2026-10-30. Built on **memory** as the one power every class draws on (the owner's choice, like
Destiny's Light). Nothing in the game has been renamed yet: the last section lists the text that would change.

## The short version

The land is forgetting itself. Fields forget how to grow, wells forget water, and people forget their own names
until there is nothing left of them but habit: the Hollow. Ashen Village is nearly gone. Maren, the one person
there who still remembers everyone, remembered **you**, and that was enough to bring you back.

You were forgotten long ago, so the forgetting has nothing left to take from you. That is your power: you can carry
what others lose. Win back what the village forgot, bring it home, and the village comes back with it.

## The power: memory

Memory is our Light: the one source behind every class, every ability and every level.

- **Souls are memories.** Everything that dies leaves some behind; you gather them, spend them, and lose them when
  you die.
- **Leveling is remembering yourself.** Resting at a bonfire, you spend what you have gathered to remember more of
  who you were: your strength (Might), your breath (Endurance), your will to live (Vigor), your hold on the power
  (Attunement).
- **The three classes are three ways of carrying it**, and none is "the" memory:
  - **Vanguard, the Ward:** remembering how to protect. Memory as a wall.
  - **Stalker, the Shade:** remembering how to hunt and hide. Memory as a shadow.
  - **Ashcaller, the Ember:** remembering the hearth. Memory as heat.
- **Bonfires** are places a memory is strong enough to hold: rest there and the land stays put long enough to
  remember you.

## The world

- **The forgetting** has no face. It's not a curse someone cast; it spreads the way neglect does, and **ash** is
  what it leaves on the ground.
- **The land reshapes itself** because it is forgotten and remembered slightly wrong, again and again. This is why
  the world is new every game. Places that are strongly remembered (the village, the bonfires, the old keeps) hold
  their shape.
- **The Hollow** are people the forgetting finished with. They still walk their old routes.

## The cast

### You

No name, no past you can recall. Maren remembers you, which is enough to wake you at the bonfire. Whether you learn
who you were is an open question (see the end of this file).

### Maren

Old, tired, stubborn. She stayed when everyone else left, and she keeps the village alive by remembering it:
names, recipes, who lived in which house. It costs her. She forgets small things now, and she is afraid of what
goes next. She sits by the village bonfire, and she is the one who tells you what to do next. She talks plainly and
briefly, and is sometimes wrong.

### The Wardens

Not villains. When the forgetting reached the village, a few people tried to save what they loved most by taking it
somewhere safe and guarding it: the smith took the forge's know-how, the fisher the river, and so on. They held on
so hard and so long that they forgot everything else, themselves included. Each Warden keeps one **remnant**: a
piece of the village's memory, which you bring home.

### The Oathbound Knights

They swore to guard the village's dead and kept the oath after they forgot why. Each knight's **oath** is the last
thing it remembers. Taking one teaches you your class ability, because an oath is memory with a purpose.

### Returning villagers

## The arc

1. **Waking.** Maren, the bonfire, the first few Hollow in the meadows. The AoE is the only power you remember.
2. **The oath** (an objective, told by Maren): the shrine knights each carry an oath; take one and you'll remember
   your class ability. Told and tracked, not marked on the map: you find a shrine yourself.
3. **The Ashen Warden**, in the keep to the north. Once Bram's apprentice (or Bram's brother; open). His remnant is
   the forge: Bram comes home and the smithy is rebuilt. You also take something of his that teaches your super.
4. **The other Wardens**, in any order, one per region. The ash recedes around the village with each one, and Maren
   remembers more.
5. **The end.** With the village remembered, the forgetting has one place left to go: wherever it started. (Open:
   see below.)

## How the story is told

- **Maren talks; the world shows.** Maren gives short spoken lines at the bonfire (and objectives). Everything else
  is in the world: signs, item descriptions, the state of the village.
- **Short and plain.** One or two sentences at a time, no speeches, no prophecy, no capitalized grand nouns beyond
  a few names.
- **Objectives** come from Maren: she tells you what she needs and why, the HUD tracks it, the map does not mark it.
- **No cutscenes.** Boss entrances are the fog gate and a title card (already in the game).

## Style

- **Plain over grand.** "The smith took the forge's know-how somewhere safe" beats "the Forgemaster sealed the
  Eternal Craft". Name few things; describe more.
- **Specific details** carry the sadness: a Hollow still carrying a water bucket, a sign with half a name.
- **Hopeful underneath.** Things come back. The village gets louder as you play.
- **US English** in all game text.

## Open questions for the owner

1. **Who were you?** Leave it unanswered, or reveal it near the end (for example, you were the one the village
   forgot first)?
2. **The ending:** where the forgetting started, and whether it can be beaten or only pushed back.
3. **The Ashen Warden's tie to Bram:** apprentice, brother, or not related.
4. **Names:** Maren, Bram, Odile, Tam, Wren and the Warden names are placeholders.
